# The AI brings the framework, never the facts

Strategy frameworks invite a model to fill quadrants with plausible but unsourced statements ("growing regulatory pressure", "competitors are moving to the cloud"). Such statements look authoritative and would reach a sponsor. So every row and item in an analysis cites its evidence: finding IDs, or a document the BA supplied (named by title and date). A statement without evidence is written as an **assumption**, marked `Assumption` in its Evidence field, and listed as an open question with the role to ask. A skill may suggest which framework headings are worth exploring and which questions to ask, but never supplies market, competitor, regulatory or financial facts itself, and never searches the web: runtimes differ in whether they can (ADR 0001) and the data policy limits what may be sent out (ADR 0006).

Numbers follow the threshold rule: only figures the sources give. A skill may calculate from given figures (totals, payback period, net present value) only when it shows the calculation and its inputs, so the BA can check it. Likelihood and impact of a risk, like requirement priority, are `TBD` until stakeholders give them.
